## A. How often a rule blocks an insertion

A stand-alone greedy allocation at planning time, not the allocation the experiments run: R4's contract-net uses its own capacity checks, and R3's rules are not called in the experiment runs. Every order is offered to every vehicle in release order and goes to the eligible vehicle whose plan gets the least extra time; a rejected pair is attributed to the rule the rule engine reports. That capacity (KR-1) is the only rule that blocks follows from how the scenarios were built: fleet capacity is sized so total demand is 80% of it, and every order is loaded at 06:00 with no unloading. It is not a measurement of R4's runs.

## B. How often a rule blocks a road (reroutes)

### B1. Road class (KR-3) for large vehicles

Large vehicles may not use residential, living_street, service or unclassified roads. The committed scenarios are small-fleet only (D2), so this measures what the rule would do. A trip is an (order node, depot) pair: 600 distinct order nodes of these scenarios x 3 hypothetical depots, placed on the large-vehicle road network (675 of 2,377 nodes). Each trip is driven by a large and a small vehicle from the same depot. Most trips are impossible because of the rule and the map together: the roads closed to large vehicles are living_street 120, residential 4,800, unclassified 2.
